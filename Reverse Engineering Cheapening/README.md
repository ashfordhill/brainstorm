# Reverse Engineering Cheapening

Reverse engineering can be very time and expertise-intensive type of task. However, with AI agents and all the built-in knowledge they've attained, now you can reverse engineer all sorts of software. 

See: https://github.com/storytold where someone used AI tools to reverse engineer for the Adobe suite. 

Legalities aside, I think software like this could be at financial risk for the future, considering that their IP is purely digital. Anything with a purely digital IP and doesn't involve raw creativity (music, art, etc.) is at a large risk for losing value. 

This also includes paid operating systems - there is a possibility of more platform-agnostic consumer software like video games coming into the world. 

People are even automating their home hubs because they are tired of the spying and multiple APIs (smart lights, switches, speakers, clocks, other tools). Why use all that and potentially be tied to a subscription system when you can have Claude develop your own all-in-one for your home hub while you sleep?

Mainly I have apprehension around the long-term viability of digital products. 

Also, Amazon reduced its weekly digital publishing limit from 10 items to 2 items. People are going to spam the hell out of digital content consumer platforms (music, videos, digital products on Amazon, Etsy, print-to-order, etc.). Which means high competition, which I personally like to avoid since it decreases success chances.

## Caveat: A Digital Idea

I do have a digital idea from time to time - currently I think there could be a use case for lonely only dwellers. People without friends (or sometimes a certain type of friends) will go to vloggers or Twitch streamers. They form relationships with others in the fandoms, they get attached to the main character to be watched. I have a really funny discord group and we always joke about how we should monetize some of the discussions. What if you could have a platform where people could randomly find a Discord server and listen in. They would not be allowed to talk but they could spend money on little effects, just like Twitch (spend 500 coins to make a fart noise, etc.). The Discord server could opt-in and it could generate income for it. People looking for Discords to browse - fun idea - either have a direct link or 'find random server'. It could be fun because people could share their favorite Discords by 'word of mouth' instead of having a typical landing page like YouTube. It'd be sorta like Omegle where the thrill or not knowing where you'll land next could be exciting. Naturally, the more entertaining servers would gain more followers than dead/not so entertaining servers.

Basically more of people selling themselves as content since that is something that AI can't do well (e.g. - generate an actually interesting interaction between multiple different people with varying personality types and backgrounds without sounding artificial). Plus people would have lives that fans could get invested in like "Oh Greg just had his first kid, wow I remember when he just graduated college, etc."

I like this idea but not really enough to prioritize it because it could be a challenge to advertise + .
